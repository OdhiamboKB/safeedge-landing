What the platform covers

A single system across the safety lifecycle, from identifying a hazard to closing out the corrective action it produced.

Risk assessments & JSAs

HIRARC, Job Safety Analysis, Safe Work Method Statements, and hazard-specific assessments (fire, environmental, COSHH), each scored on a 5×5 risk matrix with the hierarchy of controls enforced at every step.

Incident & CAPA management

Incident and near-miss reporting, bowtie barrier analysis to find which control actually failed, and a corrective-action tracker with owners, due dates and verification before closure.

Audits & inspections

Scheduled audits, field inspections and an equipment register with statutory inspection intervals and overdue tracking.

Permits & contractor management

Digital permit-to-work for high-risk activities, and contractor pre-qualification with document and certification tracking.

Training & competence

Course assignment, completion tracking and a competency matrix tied to the roles that actually need each qualification.

Compliance & management review

A legal and compliance obligations register, objectives and targets, and management review — the full management-system loop, not just a form library.

Safety thinking, not just safety paperwork

Most incident write-ups chase a single straight line of causes until they land on "the worker didn't follow procedure" — and stop there, because that's where the blame sits, not where the fix is. KB SafeEdge investigates the way barrier-based methods used in high-hazard industries do: which control was supposed to stop this, why did it fail, and what fixes that — captured as a bowtie, not a blame memo.

Gemba → go see, don't just file

Scheduled audits and field inspections against statutory and site-specific checklists, with every finding tracked to a closed-out action — not left in a notebook back at the office.

Jidoka → stop-work, encoded

A barrier marked degraded or failed on a bowtie automatically raises an owned, dated corrective action. The system doesn't let a known gap sit quietly unassigned.

Poka-yoke → engineering first

Every control on a risk assessment or bowtie is tagged by its place in the hierarchy of controls, so a plan leaning on PPE and training alone shows up as exactly that — the weaker plan.

Kaizen → participation, not a poster

Safety & health committee meetings, toolbox talks and worker-raised hazards feed the same register that management review runs on — one record, not a separate paper trail nobody reads back.
